Who Should Use CalenJob, and Who Should Pick Jobber or Housecall Pro Instead

November 11, 2026 10 min read

If you dispatch a crew, buy Jobber or Housecall Pro. If you run twenty trucks, buy ServiceTitan. If you are one person losing an hour a day to the drive between jobs, buy CalenJob. The rest of this guide explains why, and covers everyone in between.

What CalenJob actually is

CalenJob is a calendar that treats the drive between jobs as a real, scheduled event rather than empty space. Every job carries a street address, and the drive from the previous stop is calculated through Google Maps using live traffic for the hour the drive will actually happen. That drive appears on the daily timeline above the job it leads to, showing distance, duration, and the time you need to leave to arrive on schedule.

A Tuesday with four jobs does not look like four appointments separated by white space. It looks like four jobs and four drives, and how much of the day goes to the truck is visible before the day starts.

What that makes possible

The optimizer reorders the day to minimize total drive time, packs the jobs consecutively, sets a leave-by time for each one, and reports how many minutes the new order saved against the order you had. Arrival windows constrain it where they need to, so a customer who is only available between nine and eleven keeps that slot while everything else sequences around them. The lunch break is protected rather than paved over, either as a flexible window the optimizer places or a fixed time every day.

Beyond the route itself, the app handles the parts of a real day that scheduling software usually ignores. Supply house runs and personal errands go on the calendar as tasks in a different colour and are included in the optimization, because a parts run costs the same forty minutes whether or not anyone is billed for it. Buffer time adds padding between stops. A home base sets where the day starts and ends, and the commute home after the last job appears on the calendar, which is the drive most contractors forget to count when they say yes to a four o'clock call forty minutes out.

The parts that are not about driving

There is a customer book with notes and photos, so a repeat visit opens with what happened last time and what the property looks like. There is a quote builder with saved line items that exports to PDF. Jobs can repeat weekly, biweekly or monthly, and a follow-up visit can be scheduled from a finished one. Job status tracking, job search, custom colours, dark mode and weather on the dashboard are all there. Because every drive is already measured, the mileage total is a byproduct rather than a separate chore, exportable as a CSV at tax time.

One thing worth calling out, because almost nothing else in this category does it: the app keeps working without a signal. A banner says service has dropped, new jobs get an estimated drive so the day can still be sequenced, and real routes fill in once the phone reconnects. Basements and crawl spaces are where this work happens, and an app that goes blank there fails at the moment it is needed.

A fuller explanation of the approach is in What Drive Time Scheduling Actually Means.

What it deliberately does not do

There is no invoicing, no payment processing, no online booking, and no way to dispatch an employee. There is no inventory, no job costing, no marketing automation and no customer portal. That is not a list of things that are coming eventually. It is the shape of the product, and it is the reason it costs $14.99 instead of $59.

Every platform in this category started narrow and grew into a business suite, raising prices to match. Deciding what not to build is the only reason a one-person app can beat companies with hundreds of engineers at anything at all.

Who should choose CalenJob

Solo contractors whose real problem is the driving. If the day falls apart because a job in one suburb is followed by a job across the metro followed by a job back where you started, the thing you need is not a better invoice template. Plumbers, HVAC techs, electricians, appliance repair techs, locksmiths and mobile detailers all run four to eight scattered stops a day in an order nobody optimized, and that is the exact problem CalenJob was built for.

People currently running their schedule out of Google Calendar. This is the largest group and the one nobody is selling to, because every platform in this category is trying to sell a full business system instead. If a paper notebook or a phone calendar is the current system, a $14.99 app that knows how far apart the jobs are is a smaller step than a $59 platform with an onboarding process.

Anyone paying for software that does not do routing. Plenty of contractors are paying $29 to $59 a month for scheduling and client records with no geographic awareness at all. Adding CalenJob costs less than either of those subscriptions.

Contractors who want their mileage handled without a second app. Every drive is already measured, so the mileage total is a byproduct rather than a separate chore. Details are in How to Track Mileage Without a Separate App.

Two and three person crews where everyone keeps their own schedule. This works, with the caveat that there is no shared view and no assignment. Three people running three copies is three subscriptions and no coordination.

Who should not, and what to buy instead

You need to dispatch employees

If somebody who is not driving decides where the trucks go, you need a dispatch board, and CalenJob does not have one. Jobber starts at $39 a month and is the sensible default for most small residential shops. Housecall Pro starts at $59 billed annually or $79 month to month. Both handle team scheduling, customer communication, invoicing and payments in one place. The full Jobber plan breakdown is in Jobber Pricing in 2026.

Price per user is the problem, not price

Service Fusion includes unlimited users on every plan, starting around $208 a month billed annually. For one person that is an absurd number. For a ten person operation it is often cheaper than the per-user alternatives, because the monthly cost does not move when you hire. A comparison against similar mid-market tools is in Kickserv, Service Fusion, FieldEdge and Workiz compared.

QuickBooks Desktop runs your books

FieldEdge exists largely for this. Its QuickBooks Desktop integration is deeper than its competitors and that matters enormously to established HVAC and plumbing companies with a bookkeeper and twenty years of history in the file. It runs roughly $100 to $150 per user per month plus an implementation fee commonly quoted around $4,000, on an annual contract. That is the right trade for a company with a back office and the wrong one for everybody else.

You do commercial project work

Simpro is built for contractors running multi-stage installs, progress billing and real job costing across ten to five hundred technicians. Reported pricing is $150 to $300 per user per month with an implementation fee and an annual contract. If your work is quoted as projects rather than service calls, none of the products aimed at residential service will fit, including this one.

You have fifteen or more trucks

ServiceTitan is the enterprise answer, with a dispatch board, call centre tooling, inventory, memberships and marketing attribution. It is priced accordingly, commonly $250 to $498 per user per month with a five-figure implementation and a multi-year contract. Below that scale it costs more time and money than it returns. The detailed comparison is in Jobber vs ServiceTitan vs CalenJob.

You are in pest control

GorillaDesk is built around recurring routes, chemical and material tracking, and the reporting pest operators have to produce. It starts at $49 per route per month with no contract. Worth knowing that street-level routing with real drive times sits on the $99 tier rather than the entry plan. The full breakdown is in Jobber vs GorillaDesk vs CalenJob.

You haul dumpsters

Docket is waste and hauling software for companies running roughly three to fifty trucks, built around knowing where every container currently sits. That is a tracking problem no general scheduling app solves, and it is the reason this category has its own software.

Where the big platforms put their routing

The pricing is only half the story. The other half is that even when routing is included, it often cannot be used the way a solo contractor needs to use it.

Jobber's own help documentation states that route optimization has to be done on Jobber.com rather than in the Jobber app. The mobile app will show the reordered day, but it cannot generate it. For a person sitting in a van at eleven in the morning whose afternoon just changed, a feature that requires a laptop is a feature that does not exist.

The second limitation is larger. Jobber optimizes only what it calls anytime visits, meaning visits with no set start or end time. A plumber who told a customer they would arrive at ten has just made that job invisible to the optimizer. The feature works well for lawn and pest routes where nobody gets a specific time, and does very little for trades that book appointments.

GorillaDesk has a version of the same problem at the bottom of its range. Its entry plan routes by proximity rather than road data, which is a straight line between two points rather than a drive. Real street-level routing with real drive times requires the $99 tier. Simpro, at a reported $150 to $300 per user per month, does not list route optimization among its features at all and excludes GPS fleet tracking from the base product.

CalenJob optimizes from the phone, mid-day, including jobs with fixed times, because arrival windows constrain the solver rather than removing jobs from it. That is the clearest functional difference between this app and the platforms it is being compared against.

Five questions worth asking any vendor

Whichever product you end up with, these are the questions that separate a demo from reality. They are worth asking during a trial rather than after.

Can I reorder today's jobs from my phone, standing outside a customer's house? Ask them to show it on a phone, not a laptop. This is where several products quietly fail.

Will it optimize a day where every job already has a time on it? Most residential work is booked to an appointment. If the optimizer only handles jobs with no set time, it will not touch your actual schedule.

Does the calendar show me the drive between two jobs, with a time to leave? Not a map of the stops, and not a total for the day. The specific gap between job two and job three, and when to walk out the door.

What happens in a basement with no bars? Can you open the app, see the full day, add a job and mark one complete, then have it sync when you come back up. Test this one personally rather than taking an answer on trust.

What is the real monthly total? The plan, the per-user charges, the add-ons that turned out not to be included, and the card processing percentage. The advertised number and the number on the statement are frequently different here.

Running two subscriptions is a legitimate answer

Nothing here requires picking one product. Jobber at $39 for quoting, invoicing and customer records, plus CalenJob at $14.99 for the daily route, comes to about $54 a month. That is a solo operation that is both professionally run and efficiently driven, for less than a fifth of what a single ServiceTitan seat costs, and it gives both products the job they are actually good at.

The same logic applies to anyone already paying for scheduling software without routing. The question is not whether to replace it. It is whether $14.99 is worth getting back the time currently being lost to a badly ordered day. The method behind that is in How to Group Service Calls by Area.

What is coming

A Pro tier is planned after launch, built around a public booking page where customers pick their own slot and CalenJob factors in the drive from the previous job, plus payment processing and invoicing. That changes some of the answers above, because the main reason a solo contractor would currently pick Jobber over CalenJob is that Jobber handles the money and the customer-facing booking. None of it has shipped yet, which is why this article is written against what exists today rather than what is on the roadmap.

The honest summary

CalenJob does one job. If the expensive part of your week is the driving between stops, it is the cheapest way to get that under control and nothing else in this price range attempts it. If your problem is invoicing, payments, dispatching people, job costing or industry-specific compliance, buy one of the products named above, because they are better at those things and it is not close.
