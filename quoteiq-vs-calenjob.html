QuoteIQ vs CalenJob: The Cheaper Way to Get Quotes and Route Optimization

November 19, 2026 9 min read

If you're a solo contractor who wants to send quotes and invoices, collect reviews and stop losing time driving between jobs, CalenJob does all three for $18.99 a month. QuoteIQ can do it too, but the plan that includes route optimization costs $299.99. For most one-person businesses, that number settles it. Here's the rest of the comparison, including the cases where QuoteIQ is worth the extra money.

What QuoteIQ is

QuoteIQ is an all-in-one app for home service businesses, and it's especially popular with pressure washing, lawn care, window cleaning and junk removal outfits. Quoting is the heart of it. You can measure a property from satellite imagery, take photos, build an estimate, send it, and turn it into an invoice once the job is done. It runs on iPhone, Android and the web, and it leans hard on AI, from photo-to-quote tools to a virtual receptionist that answers your calls.

It's a good product, and plenty of contractors like it. So the real question isn't whether QuoteIQ is any good. It's whether a one-person operation needs most of what it charges for.

Look at that last column. Route optimization doesn't show up until Elite, at $299.99 a month, and Elite comes with ten users. If you're one person in a truck, you'd be paying for nine seats nobody ever sits in, just to get the feature that puts your stops in a sensible order.

CalenJob puts route optimization in its only plan. That's $281 a month less than QuoteIQ Elite, or almost sixteen times cheaper, for the feature a solo contractor feels every single day.

Even the cheapest QuoteIQ plan costs more. Essentials is $29.99 with no route optimization. CalenJob is $11 a month less and includes it.

Why routing is the feature worth paying for

Quoting software helps you win the job. Routing helps you make money doing it. For a lot of solo contractors, the drive between jobs is one of the biggest unpaid costs of the day, and it's the one nobody puts on the calendar.

Here's how it plays out. You book four jobs in the order the phone rang: one in the north suburbs at nine, one across town at eleven, one back near the first at two, and one somewhere in between at four. Every job is on the calendar. None of the driving is. Say a better order would cut your day's driving by 50 minutes. If your time is worth $75 an hour, that's about $62 a day, or roughly $1,250 over a 20-day month, all of it currently spent sitting in traffic.

That's the problem CalenJob was built around. Every job carries a real address, and the drive from your previous stop shows up on the calendar as its own block, with the distance, the time it takes in live traffic, and the time you need to leave. Run the optimizer and it reorders the day to cut the total drive, then tells you how many minutes it saved. Jobs with an arrival window stay inside it, and your lunch break doesn't get paved over. More on how that works is in What Is Drive Time Scheduling?

To be fair, QuoteIQ's Elite route tools are capable. They show the distance, drive time and an ETA for each stop, and its Route Density zones help you group work by area. It's a real feature. It just lives on a $299.99 plan designed for a ten-person team.

Where QuoteIQ is the better pick

CalenJob isn't the answer for everyone, and QuoteIQ earns its price in a few situations.

You need card payments in the same app today. QuoteIQ Essentials includes online payments and customer financing for $29.99. CalenJob sends invoices and receipts and tracks what's been paid, but it doesn't process cards yet. A Pro tier with card payments is planned after launch, but if you need it now, QuoteIQ has it now.

You quote from satellite images. QuoteIQ's MapMeasure Pro, on Beginner and above, lets you measure a roof, lawn or driveway without driving out to see it. For pressure washing and lawn care, that saves a lot of unpaid estimate trips.

You want an AI receptionist. QuoteIQ's virtual call team answers the phone when you can't, and it's included from the entry plan. CalenJob has nothing like it.

You run a crew. Elite adds employee tracking, dispatching and room for ten users. CalenJob is built around one person's day and has no way to assign jobs to other techs.

You're on Android or want a web version. CalenJob is iPhone only.

Getting route optimization from QuoteIQ costs roughly $2,850 a year more than getting it from CalenJob. For a one-truck business, that's a new set of tools, a few months of fuel, or most of a year's insurance.

And QuoteIQ's sticker price isn't always the whole bill. Extra users are $25 a month each. Its AI features run on monthly credits that can run out, some extras are paid add-ons, and card payments go through Stripe with a processing fee QuoteIQ doesn't publish. CalenJob's plan is one flat price.

Can you use both?

Yes, and for some people it's the smart move. If you need card payments right now, QuoteIQ Essentials plus CalenJob comes to $48.98 a month. You'd have quoting and invoicing in both apps, which is redundant, but you'd get payments from QuoteIQ and real routing from CalenJob for about a sixth of what Elite costs.

That same idea works with other tools too. A full breakdown of who should run what is in Who Should Use CalenJob, and Who Should Pick Jobber or Housecall Pro Instead.

So which one should you pick?

For most solo contractors, CalenJob is the better deal. It quotes, it invoices, it sends review requests, and it does the thing QuoteIQ saves for its $299.99 plan: it shows you the drive between every job and puts your day in the right order. It costs less than QuoteIQ's cheapest plan while doing that.

Pick QuoteIQ if you need card payments in the same app today, you quote by measuring properties from satellite, you want an AI answering your calls, or you're running a crew. Those are real strengths, and QuoteIQ handles them well.

But if you're one person, your quotes are already going out, and the thing actually eating your day is the driving, there's no reason to pay $299.99 a month to fix it.
